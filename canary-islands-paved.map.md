# FV-30 Pájara to Betancuria and the north
ride

Worth	great
Curviness	technical
Views	spectacular
Condition	smooth

The only road on Fuerteventura that turns. From Pájara it climbs through the eroded ridges of the Betancuria massif over two passes, single-lane and unguarded on the tightest bends, with the whole empty interior of the island spread out from the viewpoints at Las Peñitas and Morro Velosa, then drops into the old capital of Betancuria and on down the Santa Inés valley to the plains. Good tarmac, big drops, and little traffic beyond the tour buses.

# GC-130 Telde up to the Pico de las Nieves
ride

Worth	great
Curviness	twisty
Views	spectacular
Condition	smooth

The east-side climb to the top of the island, nearly two thousand metres up. From the last houses above Telde the road runs up through La Breña and Cazadores in long, well-surfaced curves, tightening into hairpins in the pine belt, and ends on the summit spur of the Pico de las Nieves with the caldera, the Roque Nublo and Tenerife laid out in front of it. A favourite of the riders who live on the island.

ride

Worth	good

The green way into the highlands. From the pilgrimage town of Teror the road climbs through Valleseco and a landscape of chestnut, orchard and terraced pasture that the north keeps wet and the south never sees, then turns along the rim of the Tejeda caldera to Artenara. # GC-210 La Aldea to Tejeda over Artenara
ride

Worth	epic
Curviness	punishing
Views	spectacular
Traffic	empty

A single-lane road that climbs from the west coast up the Barranco de la Aldea to the highest village on the island, past two reservoirs and through more than thirty hairpins, with a rock wall on one side and a drop on the other. Cyclists call the climb the Valley of Tears. The surface is coarse, the passing places are few, and there is almost nobody else on it. It was the location for a car-chase film, and looks it.

# LP-4 over the Roque de los Muchachos
ride

Worth	epic
Curviness	technical
Views	spectacular
Traffic	light

The highest road in the Canaries after the Teide, and the one the island's riders rate above all others. From the edge of Santa Cruz it climbs almost two and a half thousand metres through pine forest and cloud to the rim of the Caldera de Taburiente, passes the telescopes of the observatory, and drops down the other side to Garafía in the north-west. Hairpins of every shape for nearly fifty kilometres, and at the top the whole caldera at your feet and Tenerife above the clouds. Cold up there whatever the coast is doing.

# TF-21 over the Teide
ride

Worth	epic
Curviness	twisty
Views	spectacular

The island's big crossing. It leaves the green Orotava valley, climbs through the pine belt into the Las Cañadas caldera at over 2,300 metres, runs across lava fields under the volcano itself, and comes down the dry south side through Vilaflor. Nearly ninety kilometres, and the landscape changes completely three or four times on the way. Wide, well surfaced, mostly open bends; the traffic thins out the higher you go.

# TF-436 Masca road
ride

Worth	epic
Curviness	punishing
Views	spectacular
Traffic	busy

The road every rider on Tenerife has heard of before landing. From Santiago del Teide it climbs to the Cherfe saddle, then drops into the Teno massif in a stack of hairpins barely wide enough for one car, passes the hamlet of Masca on its knife-edge ridge, and climbs out again to the green north coast. Twenty-two kilometres, gradients to sixteen percent, and a view down the gorge to the sea from almost every bend. Rental cars and buses share it, so it is a road for early mornings.

# TF-82 Icod de los Vinos to Adeje over Erjos
ride

Worth	great
Curviness	twisty

The west-side link between the green north and the dry south, over the Erjos saddle. Out of Icod it climbs steeply through El Tanque, then turns into a fast, beautifully surfaced sweep of open corners over the pass and down past Santiago del Teide, Tamaimo and Chío to Adeje. German testers call the Erjos section the racing stretch of the island; it is also the natural way to reach the Masca road.
